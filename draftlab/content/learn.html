How to read this tool

  • Every number is one of three kinds. The numbers say comes from the games in your selection (tag data). The hero kit suggests comes from ability text or Icy Veins lists (tag kit or data). Coach judgment is an authored rating or opinion (tag judgment). Learn pages add source for lines taken from a named article.
  • Win rates are shrunk toward 50% with a prior of 10 games, so a hero that is 3 and 0 reads about 62%, not 100%. Map splits use a heavier prior of 20 games toward the hero's own overall rate.
  • Anything under 8 games carries a "low sample" mark. Read it as a lean. No single row in this tool is a rule.
  • Scores in Draft are a sum of named components, in points. One point is about one percentage point of win rate. Tap any suggestion to see each component, its reason, and its basis.
  • The hero matrix is a first draft. Most ratings are judgment. You can change any rating in the Heroes tab, and every tab that uses ratings follows.
  • The data cannot show timelines, talents, lanes, rotations or player skill. It cannot show why a team won. See About for the full list.

Draft format and information

  • Storm League: the map is known before the draft, each team has three bans (two early, one later), each hero can be used once, and the draft locks at the loading screen. {S:Boostroom ranked guide, Aug 2026}
  • One guide author claims the draft is worth 40 to 60 percent of the result. That is an estimate, not a measurement. {S:TL.net guide, seen through a search snippet}
  • The side with map choice can pick the map where its comp and pools are best, and the other side gets pick order value. {J}
  • Last pick sees everything, so it is the best slot for a counter or a gap fill. It only works if the pool is deep enough that a ban cannot strip it. {J}
  • Early picks reveal information. Take heroes that are strong on any comp (a flexible tank, a top healer, a hero the other side would ban late) and keep specialists for later. {J}
  • Older pro advice: first pick strong mages and good wave clear on rotation maps, then counter pick. Dated, but the logic holds. {S:GosuGamers, about 2015, dated}

Ban logic

  • Early bans go on the hero that decides games on this map, or on the opponent's best hero. When bans arrived, pros expected target banning: weak hero pools get punished. {S:Inven, three ban era}
  • Bans push drafts toward second tier heroes. When top ranged damage is banned, previously counter picked heroes see play. {S:Inven}
  • A ban can go on the best remaining support or healer, since a team can deny it with a ban and a first pick. {S:Inven}
  • A late ban answers the board: the hero that completes their comp or counters yours. {J}
  • Do not ban what a player is bad at. Check the record first. {J}

Flex picks, denial and counters

  • A flex hero fills two roles, for example a bruiser who can offlane or tank. It delays commitment so the other side cannot counter pick cleanly. A common baseline is one tank, one offlaner, one ranged assassin, one flex and one healer. {S:Boostroom ranked guide}
  • Denial means taking the strongest area damage or the best healer so the opponent cannot. It costs a pick slot, so it pays only when the hero is also good in your comp. {S:GosuGamers, about 2015, dated} {J}
  • Counters are real but usually smaller than comp and map fit. Read the reason on the hero page, not just the list. {J}
  • Counters that come from kit: sustained displacement versus a team that must line up shots, cleanse versus single target lockdown, blind or armor versus auto attack carries. {J}
  • A missing healer or front line is the hardest hole to fix late. Fragile ranged damage wants protection. {J}
  • Gap fill list before each late pick: front line, wave clear, damage, camp clear, backline protection. {S:Boostroom ranked guide}

Comp archetypes used by this tool

No Heroes specific public taxonomy was found, so these definitions are judgment. The classifier in the engine gives each hero votes for the archetypes it fits, adds team features, then subtracts the average so a score means "more of this than a typical comp". {J}

Dive
Tank plus melee assassin plus crowd control, kills a priority target fast. Beats poke and fragile backlines when it gets on them. Loses to peel heavy sustain, spread positioning and hard CC on the diver, and to wave clear siege that wins without a fight. {J}
Poke
Ranged damage and siege before the fight. Beats slow engage and objective stand offs. Loses to dive, to global or gap closing engage, and to teams that take the objective early instead of trading poke. {J}
Pick
Stuns, hooks and long range lockdown to delete one hero. Beats comps that walk in a line. Loses to teams that group tightly with cleanse and shields. {J}
Sustain or attrition
Healer stacking with tanky bruisers. Beats burst light poke and slow attrition. Loses to anti heal, big area damage and comps that win by objective rather than fights. {J}
Four man push or split
One or two heroes soak or split while the team collects camps or forces rotations. Beats teams that cannot clear waves or defend three lanes. Loses to burst that catches the lone splitter and to global presence. {J}
Deathball
Five together, big team fight talents and ultimates. Beats split and poke if it can connect. Loses to a team that keeps waves and objectives while avoiding fights, and to big area damage. {J}
Wave clear siege
Heroes that clear waves fast and siege structures from range. Beats teams that cannot hold objectives and map pressure. Loses to dive, and to a deathball that forces the fight on its terms. {J}

Archetype choice follows the map. Infernal Shrines wants area damage to clear guardians. Tomb and Cursed Hollow want wave clear and rotations. {S:Boostroom map guide}

What changes by skill level

  • Bronze to Silver: consistency over cleverness. A small hero pool, wave clear, no needless deaths, be at objectives on time. Draft mistakes here are mostly missing roles. {S:Boostroom ranked guide}
  • Gold to Platinum: map specific drafting, talent tier awareness, camp timing, cooldown tracking, punishing rotations. {S:Boostroom ranked guide}
  • Diamond to Master: draft adaptation, counter picks, vision control, precise camp timing, using talent advantages aggressively. {S:Boostroom ranked guide}
  • Pro level: bans target player pools, the draft is about denying the opponent's best heroes while keeping your own pools open, and each player has one or two heroes far above the rest. {J}
  • The skill setting in Draft changes how much easy to play heroes and low risk picks are rewarded. It is a judgment weighting, not measured. {J}
  • Common pitfalls at every level: picking only for damage while ignoring crowd control, survivability and utility; and blaming the draft after the game starts. {S:Boostroom ranked guide}

Macro concepts

Experience and soak

  • Experience is shared, so the whole team levels together. Soaking is a team job. {S:Blizzard beginner tips}
  • Soak means standing in a lane near enemy minions so the team gets XP without anyone dying. One hero in each of three lanes early. {S:Blizzard beginner tips}
  • Talent tiers unlock with team level. Level 10 is the big threshold, so do not fight behind in talent tiers. {S:Boostroom ranked guide}
  • Double soaking, with two heroes in one lane, is used when the enemy leaves a lane or to stack a quest. {S:Dignitas, about 2019}

Rotations

  • Rotate to the objective as a group while waves are cleared or being cleared by a wave clear hero. Do not leave a lane to collapse while the team gathers. {S:Boostroom map guide}
  • A missing enemy is information. If a lane hero vanishes, assume a rotation and keep the team together. {J}

Camps and tempo

  • Mercenary camps give little XP alone. The value is in timing. {S:Dignitas camp article}
  • Best window: clear camps just before an objective spawns, so the enemy must choose between defending the camp and the objective. {S:Dignitas camp article}
  • Do not grab siege and bruiser camps whenever they are free. Wait for a major objective or for the enemy to leave the lane. {S:Dignitas camp article}
  • Mistake: camping right after a successful objective push, when your lanes are already pushed into enemy territory. {S:Dignitas camp article}
  • Tempo means who forces the next decision. The team that sets up the next objective with camps, vision and cooldowns ready usually has it. {J}

Objectives, trades and level lead

  • Stacking objectives means using a lead from one objective (lanes pushed, camps taken, forts down) to be ready for the next. {S:Boostroom map guide}
  • Trading an objective for something bigger is fine. Give up an early tribute rather than lose several heroes, and treat core health on Towers of Doom as a resource. {S:Boostroom map guide}
  • Convert fights into objectives, structures or camps, not into chasing one low health survivor. {S:Boostroom ranked guide}
  • A level lead is strongest when you force fights where you have ultimates and they do not. {S:community opinion via a Reddit mirror, low quality}
  • Level leads shrink late as talents run out. Around level 20 the gap matters less than ultimate status, positioning and death timers. {J}
  • When behind, avoid even fights. Play for waves, safe camps and a defended objective until a spike. {S:Boostroom ranked guide}
  • Respawn timers were shortened in April 2026 for the mid and late game, so a won fight gives a shorter window to push. {S:patchbot.io}

Ultimate economy and calling

  • Heroics and ultimates are on long cooldowns, so tracking them is a core skill at Gold and above. {S:Boostroom ranked guide}
  • Take fights when your key ultimates are up and theirs are down. Do not waste a heroic on a fight you were going to win or lose anyway. {J}
  • One voice calls the plan and the others report information. Decide before the gates open: group, siege, gank, trap or split. {J}
  • Call targets before a fight and call off when it goes wrong. After a fight, say what to convert: objective, fort, camp or reset. {J}

Hero and combo notes

Most public hero advice reachable here is old. Treat every dated line as a hypothesis to check against the current hero page and the Heroes tab.

  • Zeratul: stealth makes enemies cautious, and Void Prison was called the best ultimate in the game in 2015. Current strength is unverified. {S:GosuGamers, about 2015, dated}
  • Nazeebo: crowd control and thoughtful ultimate choices, works in push and teamfight comps. {S:GosuGamers, about 2015, dated}
  • Gazlowe: swings between carry and useless depending on talents. Got a buff on 11 May 2026 to Rock-it Turret damage and One Man Wrecking Crew. {S:Blizzard patch notes}
  • Azmodan: controls siege objectives from range, weak to body blocking and lockdown. {S:GosuGamers, about 2015, dated}
  • Abathur: strong at splitting and global support, easy to overrate when players do not place mines or play proactively. {S:Blizzard forum thread} {J}
  • Arthas and Chen got 2026 buffs and may deserve a recheck on tight choke maps. This is commentary, not data. {S:gamer.org}
  • Tyrande plus Jaina roam early and burst one target. Malfurion plus Kael'thas wins poke wars but Kael can be burst down. Nazeebo plus Malfurion grinds comps with damage and healing over time. Zeratul with a shield hero can auto attack safely. {S:GosuGamers, about 2015, dated}
  • Two heroes that both need protection, such as two fragile siege mages, are anti synergy because the front line cannot cover both. {J}
  • A comp of only dive heroes with no wave clear loses to a siege comp without a fight. {J}
  • Mephisto wants spread fights and punishes dive. Garrosh punishes heroes who walk into his range early. {S:Icy Veins tier list snippet, 2026 status unverified}
  • Line skillshots get eaten by guardians on Infernal Shrines. {S:Dignitas}

Heroes whose value changes by map

  • Global or high mobility heroes on Cursed Hollow, Alterac Pass and Warhead Junction. {S:Boostroom map guide}
  • Area damage on Infernal Shrines, wave clear on Tomb and Cursed Hollow, sustain and duelists on Braxis Holdout. {S:Boostroom map guide}
  • Range siege heroes on siege objective maps, and splitters on three lane maps such as Garden of Terror and Alterac Pass. {J}

Common misplays by role

  • Tank: dives in alone with no follow up, ignores soak and rotations, uses an engage ultimate with no one in range. {J}
  • Healer: stands in front, spends mana on poke so none is left for the fight, heals the person who is not in danger. {J}
  • Ranged assassin: overextends for one more auto attack, ignores the wave and gets punished by dive. {J}
  • Bruiser or offlaner: stays in a lane too long after level 10 and misses the fight, or leaves a lane open before an objective. {J}
  • Specialist: not using the global or split tools the hero exists for. {J}
  • All roles: playing too many heroes and never learning matchups, fighting behind in talent tiers, copying builds without understanding them. {S:Boostroom ranked guide}

Glossary of hero attributes

Scale for ratings: 1 poor, 2 below average, 3 average, 4 strong, 5 elite among all heroes. For ease of execution, 5 means easy to play well.

Camp clear
Solo mercenary camp speed.
Wave clear
How fast and safely the hero clears minion waves.
Teamfight
Impact in a five against five.
Engage
Starts or forces fights.
Peel
Protects allies from dive.
Self sustain
Survives without a healer.
Mobility
Repositioning and rotation speed.
Ease of execution
How easy it is to play well. The Skill setting in Draft rewards or discounts this.
Objective burst
Burns bosses, shrines and structures fast.
Siege
Takes down towers and forts safely.
Damage profile
Burst, sustain or mixed. Percent damage means a base ability or talent deals a share of maximum health to heroes.
Range class
By auto attack: melee, short (about 3 to 5), medium (about 6) or long (7 or more).
Power spike
Early, mid (levels 10 to 13), late (20) or flat.
Pick risk
Safe, medium or high risk if the pick goes wrong.
Basis tags
kit read from ability text, data from games or Icy Veins lists, judgment authored. Confidence (low, med, high) sits next to the tag in the Heroes tab.
Shrunk win rate
(wins + 5) divided by (games + 10). It pulls small samples toward 50%.
Lift
A head to head or pair result minus what the two heroes' own win rates predict.
